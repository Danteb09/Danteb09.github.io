Manjar Crepes (Panqueques de Manjar)

Click here to skip to recipe

Manjar, also known as dulce de leche in Argentina or Spain, is a big component of Chilean pastries and sweets. My dad once said: I don't ever crave sweets, but I do really like manjar. My favorite application of this sweet cream is in crepes, which I first had from my grandma when I was very young and still living in Chile. This was typically a light evening meal—or "once," as we called it there—served with a tea to balance out its sweetness. We still make these here in the US regularly, but when we make them, they don't last very long.

Ingredients

Quantity Ingredient
2 cups (and a splash) Milk
2 cups Flour
2 Eggs
1 can (14 ounces) Dulce de Leche
1 tsp Salt

Materials
Measuring Cup
Medium Frying Pan
2 large plates
Spoon
Deep Spoon/Ladle
Large Mixing Bowl
Whisk
Spatula

Instructions

  1. Mix eggs and milk in a large bowl
  2. Whisk flour into mixture
  3. Open can of dulce de leche and mix splashes of milk into it until it creamy and easily spreadable
  4. Pour a thin layer of batter into a frying pan on high heat and cook both sides
  5. Put cooked crepe on a large plate
  6. Repeat steps 4 and 5 until all batter is used up
  7. Spread dulce de leche onto the topmost crepe
  8. Roll up the crepe and set it onto a serving plate
  9. Repeat steps 7 and 8 until all crepes are made

Makes 12 rolled crepes
Estimated time to create: 28 minutes (10 to prepare batter and dulce de leche, 1.5 per crepe)

Extra Tips

  • Add 3 drops of vanilla extract to the batter to add an extra flavor
  • Sprinkle powdered sugar onto the crepes for a sweet topping
  • Make sure that the pan is completely heated up before pouring batter
  • Pairs great with tea
  • Can't find canned dulce de leche?
    1. Submerge and boil a can of sweetened condensed milk for 3 hours
    2. Let can cool to room temperate
